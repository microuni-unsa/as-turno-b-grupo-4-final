# ISO/IEC 27701:2025

## Clause 4.2 Understanding the needs and expectations of interested parties
The organization shall determine:
a) interested parties that are relevant to the privacy information management system;
b) the relevant requirements of these interested parties;
c) which of these requirements will be addressed through the privacy information management system.
NOTE The requirements of interested parties can include legal, regulatory, contractual and supervisory requirements regarding the protection of PII.

## Clause 5.3 Roles, responsibilities and authorities
Top management shall ensure that the responsibilities and authorities for relevant roles are assigned and communicated within the organization.
Top management shall assign the responsibility and authority for:
a) ensuring that the privacy information management system conforms to the requirements of this document;
b) reporting on the performance of the privacy information management system to top management.

## Clause 6.1.2 Privacy risk assessment
The organization shall define and apply a privacy risk assessment process that:
a) establishes and maintains privacy risk criteria that include:
1) the risk acceptance criteria; and
2) criteria for performing privacy risk assessments;
b) ensures that repeated privacy risk assessments produce consistent, valid and comparable results;
c) identifies the privacy risks:
1) apply the privacy risk assessment process to identify risks associated with the loss of confidentiality, integrity and availability for personal identifiable information (PII) within the scope of the privacy information management system;
2) identify the risk owners;
d) analyses the privacy risks:
1) assess the realistic likelihood of occurrence;
2) assess the potential consequences for PII principals should those risks materialize;
e) evaluates the privacy risks:
1) compare the results of risk analysis with the risk criteria;
2) prioritize the analyzed risks for risk treatment.

## Clause 6.1.3 Privacy risk treatment
The organization shall define and apply a privacy risk treatment process to treat risks related to the processing of PII, including risks to PII principals, and including the security of PII, by:
a) selecting appropriate privacy risk treatment options, taking account of the risk assessment results;
b) determining all controls that are necessary to implement the privacy risk treatment option(s) chosen;
c) identifying and documenting the information security and privacy programme implemented by the organization, including the appropriate security controls;
The information security programme at a minimum should address:
— information security risk management;
— policies for information security;
— organization of information security;
— human resources security;
— asset management;
— access control;
— operations security;
— network security management;
— incident management;
— information security reviews;
— physical and environmental security.
d) comparing the controls determined with those in Annex A and Annex B, verifying that no necessary controls have been omitted;
e) producing a Statement of Applicability (SoA) that contains the necessary controls, justification for inclusions and justifications for exclusions.

## Clause 6.13 Information security incident management
The organization shall establish, document, implement and maintain processes and procedures for information security incident management to ensure a quick, effective and orderly response to information security events and incidents.

### Clause 6.13.1.1 Incident management planning and preparation
The organization shall define and allocate information security incident management roles, responsibilities and procedures. The organization shall establish incident handling procedures to plan and prepare for managing information security incidents effectively and in a timely manner.

### Clause 6.13.1.2 Reporting information security events
Information security events shall be reported through appropriate management channels as quickly as possible. All personnel and contractors shall be made aware of their responsibility to report any observed or suspected information security events in systems or services.

### Clause 6.13.1.3 Reporting information security weaknesses
Personnel and contractors shall be required to note and report any observed or suspected information security weaknesses in systems or services without undue delay.

### Clause 6.13.1.4 Assessment and decision on information security events
Information security events shall be assessed and it shall be decided if they are to be categorized as information security incidents. Events involving personal identifiable information (PII) shall trigger an assessment to determine if a PII breach has occurred.

### Clause 6.13.1.5 Response to information security incidents
Information security incidents shall be responded to in accordance with the documented procedures. Response actions shall include containment, eradication, forensic investigation, escalation, and internal and external communications.

### Clause 6.13.1.6 Learning from information security incidents
Knowledge gained from analyzing and resolving information security incidents shall be used to identify recurring or high impact incidents and to improve information security controls.

### Clause 6.13.1.7 Collection of evidence
The organization shall define and apply procedures for the identification, collection, acquisition, and preservation of information that can serve as evidence. For incidents involving potential legal action or regulatory notification, evidence shall be collected and preserved in a forensically sound manner.

## Clause 8.2 Privacy risk assessment
The organization shall perform privacy risk assessments at planned intervals or when significant changes are proposed or occur, taking account of the criteria established in Clause 6.1.2. Documented information of the results of the privacy risk assessments shall be retained.

## Clause 9.2 Internal audit
The organization shall conduct internal audits at planned intervals to provide information on whether the privacy information management system:
a) conforms to:
— the organization's own requirements for its privacy information management system;
— the requirements of this document;
b) is effectively implemented and maintained.
The organization shall plan, establish, implement and maintain an internal audit programme, including frequency, methods, responsibilities, planning requirements and reporting. Results of audits and nonconformity findings shall be reported to relevant managers.

## Clause 9.3.2 Management review inputs
The management review shall include consideration of:
a) the status of actions from previous management reviews;
b) changes in external and internal issues that are relevant to the privacy information management system;
c) changes in needs and expectations of interested parties that are relevant to the privacy information management system;
d) information on the privacy information management system performance, including trends in:
— nonconformities and corrective actions;
— monitoring and measurement results;
— audit results;
e) opportunities for continual improvement.

## Clause 10.2 Nonconformity and corrective action
When a nonconformity occurs, the organization shall:
a) react to the nonconformity, and as applicable:
— take action to control and correct it;
— deal with the consequences;
b) evaluate the need for action to eliminate the cause(s) of the nonconformity, in order that it does not recur or occur elsewhere, by:
— reviewing the nonconformity;
— determining the causes of the nonconformity;
— determining if similar nonconformities exist, or can potentially occur;
c) implement any action needed;
d) review the effectiveness of any corrective action taken;
e) make changes to the privacy information management system, if necessary.
Corrective actions shall be appropriate to the effects of the nonconformities encountered. Documented information shall be retained as evidence of the nature of the nonconformities and subsequent actions taken, and the results of any corrective action.

## Control A.3.11 Incident management planning and preparation
The organization shall plan and prepare for managing information security incidents related to PII processing by defining, establishing and communicating incident management processes, roles and responsibilities. The organization shall establish procedures to identify and record events and determine if a PII breach has occurred.

## Control A.3.12 Response to information security incidents
Responses to information security incidents related to PII processing shall be according to the documented procedures. Responses shall include containment, communication with affected parties, notification of competent supervisory authorities where required, and execution of corrective action plans.

## Control B.3.11 Incident management planning and preparation
The organization should plan and prepare for managing information security incidents related to PII processing by defining, establishing and communicating incident management processes, roles and responsibilities.
As part of the overall information security incident management process, the organization should establish responsibilities and procedures for identifying and recording breaches of PII. Additionally, the organization should establish responsibilities and procedures related to notifying relevant parties of PII breaches (including the timing of such notifications) and the disclosure to authorities, taking into account the applicable legal requirements.

## Control B.3.12 Response to information security incidents
Responses to information security incidents related to PII processing should be according to the documented procedures.
Implementation guidance for PII controllers:
An incident that involves PII should trigger a review by the organization, as part of its information security incident management process, to determine if a breach involving PII that requires a response has taken place.
When a breach of PII has occurred, response procedures should include relevant notifications and records.
Where a breach involving PII has occurred, a record should be maintained with sufficient information to provide a report for regulatory or forensic purposes, such as:
— description of the incident;
— the time period;
— consequences of the incident;
— the name of the reporter;
— to whom the incident was reported;
— steps taken to resolve the incident (including the person in charge and the data recovered);
— the fact that the incident resulted in unavailability, loss, disclosure or alteration of PII.
In the event that a breach involving PII has occurred, the record should also include a description of the PII compromised, if known. If notifications were used, the steps taken to notify PII principals, regulatory agencies or customers should also be recorded.
Implementation guidance for PII processors:
In some jurisdictions, the PII processor should notify the PII controller of the existence of a breach without undue delay (i.e. as soon as it is discovered), so that the PII controller can take the appropriate actions. Where a breach involving PII has occurred, a record should be maintained with sufficient information to provide a report for regulatory or forensic purposes, including description of the incident, consequences of the incident, and steps taken to resolve.
